The science behind evidence

What is forensic science?

Forensic science applies scientific methods to questions related to law and investigations. It helps examine physical and biological evidence objectively so that findings can support an investigation or a court of law.

Explore the fields

Major forensic disciplines

Different types of evidence require different scientific methods. Here are six important areas.

🖐️

Fingerprint Examination

Studies friction ridge patterns to compare and evaluate impressions found at a scene.

🧬

Forensic Biology & DNA

Examines biological materials such as blood, saliva and hair using scientific techniques.

🔬

Forensic Chemistry

Analyses unknown substances, residues and materials to understand their composition.

📄

Questioned Documents

Examines handwriting, signatures, inks, paper and possible alterations in documents.

⚖️

Forensic Toxicology

Detects and interprets drugs, alcohol and toxic substances in biological samples.

📷

Crime Scene Investigation

Focuses on documenting, collecting, preserving and packaging evidence carefully.

Learn through examples

Evidence in action

These fictional learning examples show how forensic disciplines can contribute to an investigation.

Case file 01 — The glass fragment

A broken window is found at a scene. Investigators document the fragments and a laboratory may compare their physical and chemical properties with a questioned sample.

Case file 02 — The anonymous note

A note is recovered during an inquiry. A document examiner may study handwriting characteristics, writing materials and signs of alteration.

Case file 03 — The unknown stain

A reddish stain is located and collected using proper procedures. Laboratory screening and confirmatory tests can help determine its nature.
